How Five Patent-Pending Inventions Came From One Observation

01 — The Gap Nobody Was Filling

I noticed something that haunted me for years: nobody was automatically connecting environmental triggers to physiological responses in real time.

We had sensors. We had AI. We had cloud infrastructure. Yet when someone developed a respiratory issue indoors, nobody knew why — not in the moment, not with legal rigor. The medical record said "patient reported shortness of breath at 14:32." The facility log said "HVAC alarm at 14:28." But they were in different systems. Nobody was automating the connection.

02 — The First Invention

So I built CEHE — Causal Environmental Health Event detection. Real-time environmental sensor data correlated with real-time physiological data. Automatic causal inference. Legally defensible, timestamped, tamper-evident records. Output structured for medicolegal documentation, disability claims, occupational health audits.

03 — One Problem, Four Layers

4

Layer 1: Detection (CEHE). Layer 2: Prediction (ML). Layer 3: Geographic distribution (Network). Layer 4: Clinical integration (EHR). Each layer unlocks a different use case. Together they form an ecosystem.

I filed all four as separate patents on the same day: April 13, 2026.

The AQD Method™

A framework for independent invention. From observation to patent-pending portfolio in months, not years.

1

Observe a Specific Gap

Not a vague market opportunity. A failure mode you've seen repeatedly. Something that breaks predictably, costs people money or health, and nobody's automating it.

2

Define the Failure Mode in Writing

Document exactly what breaks. Who it affects. What the current workaround costs. Make it concrete enough that someone else would nod and say "yes, that's a real problem."

3

Build the Minimum System That Detects It

Not a product. Not a business. A working prototype that proves the failure mode can be detected. Use off-the-shelf components. Get real data.

4

Layer Solutions for Different Scales

One invention rarely solves the whole problem. Think in layers: detection, prediction, distribution, integration. File each as a separate invention if they're independent.

5

Document Obsessively

Every decision. Every test result. Every failure. This becomes your patent specification. Write as if defending your invention to a skeptical engineer — precision over persuasion.

6

File Early. Use Provisional Patents.

You don't need a finished product or a lawyer. Provisional patents are $80 per Micro Entity, time-stamped, and give you one year to refine before going non-provisional.

7

Iterate Based on Real-World Performance

Your provisional filing is the beginning. Use your one-year window to gather feedback, test in production, refine your claims. Go non-provisional with validation behind you.
